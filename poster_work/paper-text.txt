Overcoming Template Mega Clusters and Pooling Cancellation in
Unsupervised Incident Triage
Anonymous Author(s)
Abstract
Enterprise helpdesks and software repositories receive thousands
of unstructured incident reports each quarter, and operations teams
need to know which tickets belong together and which problems
are growing. In our enterprise Information Technology Service
Management (ITSM) benchmark, nearly one in four tickets (314 of
1,380 records) sit orphaned in an Unassigned backlog without a
resolution timestamp because front line Tier 1 intake agents cannot
determine which specialist team owns cross boundary security,
network, or identity outages. Meanwhile, open source maintainers
struggle to distinguish genuine user bug outbreaks from internal
refactoring pull requests (PRs). We show that standard unsuper-
vised topic modeling falls into two traps on these streams. In the
Template and Mega Cluster Trap, word coherence metrics (𝐶𝑣)
reward models that group recurring ticket boilerplate, while default
density clustering merges distinct software bugs into one mega
cluster. In theStatic Window Trap, comparing two fixed periods
suffers frompooling cancellation(a drop in one month and a rise
in the next cancel out once the months are combined) and cannot
tell a short internal coding sprint from a chronic bug plateau. We
present a three stage framework that avoids both traps: domain
pruned term weighting with size bounded hierarchical density clus-
tering (Bounded EOM) for discovery, false discovery rate (FDR)
tests with 14 day rolling windows for shift tracking, and a check
against withheld operational metadata. On open source GitHub
issues and enterprise service desk tickets, the framework splits a
298 ticket mega cluster into five actionable subsystems, reveals four
enterprise shifts that coarse windows erased, separates internal
maintainer sprints from external user bug outbreaks, and groups
75.5% of orphaned enterprise incidents with similar tickets that a
team already handled.

1 Introduction
In enterprise Information Technology Service Management (ITSM),
incoming support contacts are triaged through a tiered queue hier-
archy [1, 6]: front lineTier 1intake agents handle routine fulfillment
requests and initial categorization, escalating unresolved technical
faults to generalTier 2support technicians or specialized engi-
neering queues (such as network operations, endpoint security,
or identity management). While routine requests (such as provi-
sioning a software license or scheduling training) map cleanly to
deterministic fulfillment workflows, complex infrastructure outages
often produce ambiguous user symptoms that mask the underlying
subsystem [8]. When a Tier 1 agent cannot identify the owning
specialist department, tickets bounce across repeated queue reas-
signments or stagnate in generic holding queues. In a synthetic
enterprise helpdesk benchmark [6], this diagnostic ambiguity leaves
314 error incidents (nearly one quarter of all incoming volume, al-
most half of them high or critical priority) completely orphaned in
an Unassigned backlog without a recorded resolution. A parallel
triage challenge arises in open source Site Reliability Engineering
(SRE) and software maintenance [12, 16], where maintainers ingest
a mixed stream of external user bug reports and internal developer
pull requests (PRs) and must rapidly isolate postrelease regressions
from routine refactoring [8].
Unsupervised topic discovery [5, 9, 11] offers a natural path to
automate both intake routing and outbreak surveillance without re-
quiring labeled training histories. By grouping semantically related
tickets into clusters and tracking each cluster’s prevalence over
time, an operations team should be able to dispatch unassigned
tickets to specialist queues and detect emerging failure surges as
they form. Yet when practitioners apply standard topic modeling
and drift monitoring pipelines to technical ticket streams, they
encounter two systematic failure modes.
First, standard clustering pipelines fall into theTemplate and
Mega Cluster Trap. Nearly every support ticket or bug issue con-
tains recurring submission scaffolding such as hello team , error
message, or dataset, python, line . Classical bag of words mod-
els such as Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) [5] group documents
around these high frequency template words. Because template
tokens cooccur across almost every sliding window in the cor-
pus, LDA achieves deceptively high automated word coherence
scores (𝐶𝑣) [20, 22] while mixing unrelated technical subsystems
into the same topic [13]. Neural pipelines following the BERTopic
paradigm [11], which encode text with Sentence-BERT (SBERT)
transformers [21, 25], project embeddings via Uniform Manifold Ap-
proximation and Projection (UMAP) [19], and extract dense clusters
via Hierarchical Density Based Spatial Clustering of Applications
with Noise (HDBSCAN) [7, 18], overcome lexical sparsity [4, 24].

However, when distinct software failure modes share low level stack
trace vocabulary (such as pickle, dtype, and arrow), HDBSCAN’s
default Excess of Mass (EOM) tree selection [7] overmerges them
into a single ancestor “mega cluster” that obscures which specific
subsystem is failing.
Second, standard temporal monitors fall into theStatic Window
Trap. Drift studies commonly partition a ticket stream into two
static eras (a baseline window and an observation window) and
rank topics by their proportional shift [8, 9, 11]. When a topic dips
in one month and surges in the next, and both months fall inside the
same observation window, the dip and the surge cancel out once the
window is pooled. We call thispooling cancellation: the pooled
share looks unchanged, hiding a major workload shift. Conversely,
when a positive two period surge is detected, static windowing
cannot tell whether the shift reflects a chronic bug plateau that
persisted all quarter or a short internal coding sprint that already
resolved.
We overcome both traps through the three stage framework
illustrated in Figure 1 and evaluate it on open source software
engineering issues [16] and enterprise IT service desk tickets [6].
Specifically, we: (1) explain and resolve the coherence mirage and
mega cluster overmerging using domain pruned term weighting
and size bounded HDBSCAN tree selection (Bounded EOM); (2)
pair Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) contingency
testing [2, 10] with continuous 14 day rolling trajectories [15, 17] to
expose shifts hidden by pooling cancellation and separate chronic
plateaus from transient spikes; and (3) validate our clusters against
withheld contributor roles and helpdesk routing logs, separating
internal developer sprints from external user bug outbreaks and
grouping orphaned enterprise incidents with similar tickets that a
team already handled.
2 Related Work
Topic Modeling on Short Technical Text.Generative topic
models such as LDA [ 5] and Dynamic Embedded Topic Models
(DETM) [9] model documents as mixtures of multinomial word dis-
tributions. On short technical tickets dominated by stack traces and
version identifiers, modular neural pipelines that decouple represen-
tation learning from keyword extraction consistently outperform
generative bag of words priors [4, 11, 24]. Following BERTopic [11],
such pipelines combine transformer embeddings [21, 25], UMAP
manifold projection [19], HDBSCAN clustering [7, 18], and class
based TF-IDF (c-TF-IDF) term weighting. While Hoyle et al. [13]
showed that automated sliding window coherence metrics [20, 22]
correlate poorly with human judgment on general corpora, we
identify the specific mechanism driving coherence failure on tem-
plated tickets and show how bounding HDBSCAN branch selection
prevents parent mega clusters from swallowing distinct failure
modes.
Automated Triage and Temporal Drift Monitoring.In soft-
ware maintenance and service desk operations, syntax tree log
parsers such as Drain [12] and supervised ticket classifiers [1, 8]
require fixed log templates or labeled training histories, preventing
them from routing unlabeled queues or isolating novel postrelease

tions across discrete time buckets [9, 11], but rely on uncalibrated

3 Methodology
3.1 Overview and Problem Formulation
In plain terms, the pipeline follows each ticket through three stages.
Stage 1 turns the ticket text into a vector, places similar tickets near
each other, and groups them into clusters, keeping each cluster small
enough to describe one specific failure. Stage 2 counts how many
tickets each cluster receives over time and asks whether a change
is larger than chance, both between two periods and in a sliding 14
day window. Stage 3 compares the clusters with information the
pipeline never saw, such as who wrote each ticket and which team
handled it. Throughout the paper,R clusters are the original GitHub
clusters, C clusters are the finer GitHub clusters after Bounded EOM,
andITclusters come from the enterprise service desk.

4 Experimental Setup
4.1 Benchmark Corpora
To evaluate generalization across distinct operational environments,
we test our framework on two timestamped corpora (Figure 1). First,
the open source software engineering (SWE) corpus from the Hug-
ging Face datasets GitHub repository [ 16] comprises 𝑁= 881
records across Q1 2021 (478records) and Q2 2021 (403records),
pairing issue (41.7%) and pull request (58.3%) text with withheld
GitHub author_association roles (MEMBER, CONTRIBUTOR, or ex-
ternal community user NONE), maintainer tags, and Mean Time to
Resolution (MTTR, elapsed hours from opening to closure). Second,
the enterprise IT service desk benchmark [6], a publicly released
synthetic dataset of IT call center tickets, contains 𝑁= 1, 380
records spanning Q1 2021 (1, 338tickets across January, February,
and March) plus a multiyear 2024 slice (42tickets), with curated
ground truth labels for ticket type (Incident versus Request), cat-
egory, subcategory, priority, reassignment count (queue hops), 15
specialist support queues, and314unassigned Q1 error incidents.
On this corpus, the model benchmark in Table 1 compares both
standard EOM (𝐾= 14) and Bounded EOM ( 𝐾= 22). Because
the IT stream has no mixed mega cluster under standard EOM (its
largest cluster is a single homogeneous software installation queue,
and the five critical outage families already reach93 .8%to100%
subcategory purity), the shift analysis (Section 6.2) and the triage
evaluation (Section 7.2) use the standard EOM clusters (𝑚cl = 20,
𝑚pts = 6, labeled IT00 to IT13) to retain higher ticket coverage
(14.9%noise versus34 .5%under Bounded EOM, where the 120
ticket cap subdivides large routine queues).
4.2 Baselines and Evaluation Metrics
We benchmark our unbounded EOM and Bounded EOM pipelines
against three baselines across matching cluster counts 𝐾∈
{10, 14, 22} under both standard English and domain pruned
stopwords: classical generative LDA [ 5], sparse TF-IDF with 𝐾
Means (TF-IDF+KM), and dense 384D Sentence-BERT embeddings
with𝐾 Means (SBERT+KM) [21]. Following Röder et al. [22], we in-
dex word cooccurrences over a sliding 110 token window (stride 25)
to compute Normalized Pointwise Mutual Information (NPMI) [20],
which measures how much more frequently two words cooccur
than expected under independence, and 𝐶𝑣 coherence, which
averages the cosine similarity between each top word’s NPMI
context vector and the topic’s aggregate vector. We also report
Topic Diversity (Div., unique fraction among top 10 words across all
topics), Topic Quality (𝐶𝑣×Div.), and Silhouette coefficients [23] in
384D (Sil.384) and 5D UMAP space (Sil.5D), which contrast within
cluster cohesion against nearest cluster separation on[−1, 1] over
the assigned non noise tickets (with the 𝐶−1 share reported as
Noise in Table 1). Extrinsic accuracy on enterprise ground truth is
measured via Cluster Purity (fraction of assigned tickets matching
their cluster’s majority ground truth class) and Adjusted Rand
Index (ARI, chance corrected pairwise assignment agreement on
[−1,1]) [14].

Mirage and Mega Clusters
5.1 Why High Word Coherence (𝐶 𝑣) Is a Mirage
on Ticket Streams
Across both operational benchmarks, Table 1 reveals an inverse
relationship between bag of words coherence and geometric or op-
erational validity. Lexical bag of words baselines achieve the highest
𝐶𝑣 coherence across all blocks of Table 1, led by classical LDA on
SWE (0.948standard;0 .910pruned) and by sparse TF-IDF+𝐾 Means
(0.937) followed by LDA (0.905) on IT, yet both baselines suffer neg-
ative or near zero 5D manifold Silhouette scores (−0.225to0 .058)
while LDA ranks last on every enterprise ground truth metric.
This paradox arises from how sliding window NPMI evaluates
word cooccurrence in templated text [ 13]. Because submission
boilerplate (dataset, github, python, error message ) appears
in nearly every ticket, LDA places slight permutations of these
ubiquitous tokens at the top of multiple topics, depressing Topic
Diversity (0.670). Because boilerplate words cooccur across almost
every 110 token window, their pairwise NPMI is artificially inflated,
rewarding LDA with a high𝐶𝑣 score even though documents shar-
ing template words describe unrelated technical failures and scatter
across the embedding space.
Stripping domain boilerplate (middle and bottom blocks
of Table 1) forces c-TF-IDF to rank subsystem specific identi-
fiers ( duplicatedkeyserror, prepare_for_task, crowdstrike
falcon). Because specialized identifiers appear only within narrow
failure modes, corpus wide window cooccurrence drops, lowering
𝐶𝑣 while lifting Topic Diversity above0.94. Crucially, comparing𝐾
Means against Bounded EOM on the enterprise stream reveals why
density clustering excels at fine grained triage: whereas forced𝐾
Means partitioning assigns every ambiguous ticket to its nearest

5.2 Breaking the 298 Ticket “Mega Cluster”
with Bounded EOM
Manifold projection must precede density clustering: in raw 384D
SBERT space, cosine distance concentration [ 3] flattens density
differences so severely that HDBSCAN rejects98.2%of the corpus
as noise, whereas the 5D projection restores the dense regions that
clusters need.
Within this 5D manifold, however, comparing unbounded EOM
(𝐾= 10, clusters R0 to R9) against Bounded EOM (𝐾= 14, clusters
C0 to C13, max_cluster_size= 120) exposes a critical hierarchi-
cal overmerging effect [7, 16]. Left unconstrained, a single parent
cluster (R1) absorbs33 .8%of the entire GitHub corpus (46 .5%of
assigned records). Cross tabulating cluster memberships across the
condensed tree reveals that Bounded EOM leaves the nine special-
ized clusters ( R0, R2 to R9) completely unchanged ( ARI= 1.000
outside R1), while splitting the 298 ticket R1 ancestor into five
mechanically distinct subsystems in Figure 2 and Table 2: multi-
processing worker pool .map() serialization (pickle) locks ( C5,
𝑁= 92), Apache Arrow columnar int64 memory mapped index
collisions (C7,𝑁= 35), PyArrow C++ Application Binary Interface
(ABI) and Conda wheel conflicts (C3,𝑁= 23), tensor format cast-
ing (C2,𝑁= 31), and tokenizer C++ _libs bindings (C8,𝑁= 21).
Because all five failure modes share low level stack trace tokens
(pickle, dtype, arrow), their branches merge near the root of the
condensed tree; enforcing the Bounded EOM size cap selects the
descendant branches instead, cutting maximum cluster share nearly
in thirds (46.5%to16 .9%). We now examine how their prevalence
evolves across coarse and continuous time horizons.

8 Limitations
Our study covers one open source repository and one enterprise
benchmark, and the enterprise tickets are synthetic, so results
on real helpdesks and other organizations may differ. Several
clusters are small (for example, R3 has 20 tickets), and GitHub
author_association roles are only a proxy for whether a contrib-
utor is internal. Finally, we fit the clusters on the full study period;
running the pipeline online on new tickets without refitting is left
for future work.
9 Conclusion
Turning unstructured ticket streams into reliable operational in-
telligence requires overcoming the lexical and temporal traps that
distort standard topic models. Across open source and enterprise
benchmarks, we showed that: (1) sliding window coherence (𝐶𝑣)
